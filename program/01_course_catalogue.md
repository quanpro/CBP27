### Course cards (objectives drive the checkpoint items)

**A2 · Data to Decisions** (Priority 1 · Cycle 1 · Jan–Feb)
- Business problem: planners spend hours each week copying SAP extracts into Excel by hand; reports break and numbers disagree.
- After this course you can: (1) shape data as a clean table (one row per record, one column per field, no merged cells); (2) look up and summarise with XLOOKUP, SUMIFS and a PivotTable; (3) build a Power Query that imports, cleans, merges/appends, unpivots and refreshes; (4) choose the chart that answers the question and avoid misleading axes; (5) read a Power BI page with slicers and one measure.
- Field: rebuild one weekly report in Power Query; prove it refreshes in under 5 minutes. KPI: hours saved per week (team target ≥ 10 h/week by Dec).
- Forum (with A3): "Data & AI Lab" — teams rebuild the same messy weekly report, race the refresh, then let Copilot draft the commentary.

**A3 · AI at Work** (Priority 1 · Cycle 1 · Jan–Feb)
- Business problem: AI is available (Microsoft 365 Copilot) but used rarely or unsafely; people do not know what it is good for in planning.
- After this course you can: (1) use Copilot inside the company guideline (company account only; no customer prices, contracts, salaries or personal data in prompts unless IT confirms protection; never paste company data into public tools); (2) write a prompt with role, task, context, format; (3) use Copilot in Outlook, Teams, Excel and PowerPoint for 3+ of your own tasks; (4) verify every number Copilot produces against the source; (5) know when not to use AI (decisions, sign-offs, anything you cannot check).
- Field: three use cases in your own job; save your best prompt to the team library; log time saved. KPI: 100% weekly Copilot users; hours saved.

**A1 · The Right Question** ✅ (Priority 2 · Cycle 2 · Feb–Mar)
- Method and names exactly as Quân's deck "Problem-Solving, The One Skill That Changes Everything": 1 Define the Problem · 2 Break Problem into Issues · 3 Eliminate non key issues · 4 Issue analysis and workplan · 5 Conduct work items · (6–7 in C1) · ITERATE.
- After this course you can: (1) write a problem as a gap with numbers and a date; (2) pass a statement through the six characteristics (specific and measurable, time-bound, a question not a fact, debatable, actionable, what the decision-maker needs); (3) build a MECE logic tree and pick the right tree type; (4) prioritise with 80:20 and Size of Prize × Easy to capture; (5) turn an issue analysis into a 2–4-week work plan with owners and end products; (6) name the five biases.
- Pre-work: Plant B cold case (built page). Forum (the A1 blueprint's Forum 1; its Forum 2 becomes the 45-minute charter clinic at the P1 forum plus the monthly Case Clinic): solve Plant B, then a topic Quân chooses; charter each team project as a one-day answer (SOI) + issue tree. Field: charter signed off by the manager. Rule from Apr 2027: any issue at S&OE, DSR or S&OP comes with a one-page problem statement.

**C1 · Effective Communication** ✅ (Priority 2 · Cycle 4 · May–Jun → Pitch Day)
- Steps 6–7 from Quân's deck "Effective Communication: Story-Telling and Kick-ass Presentation". Tagline: Answer First.
- After this course you can: (1) ask "so what" three times to synthesise; (2) write the one-day answer as SOI (Situation, Observation incl. complication and question, Implication); (3) build a pyramid with vertical and horizontal logic; (4) write top-down emails and action titles; (5) choose the chart for the comparison (component, item, time series, frequency, correlation).
- Field: three real emails rewritten top-down; one recurring pack with a one-page executive summary and action titles; charter deck ≤ 8 slides. Offline session: **Pitch Day** (project mid-point, VPSC on the panel); its first hour is the storyline clinic that the C1 blueprint had as a separate "Build the pyramid" forum. Rule from Jul 2027: action title on every S&OP/DSR slide; every pack opens with a one-page executive summary.

**P1 · Line of Sight · Capacity Planning** ✅ (Priority 3 · Cycle 3 · Apr–May)
- After this course you can: (1) speak factory (site, line, bottleneck, buffers); (2) convert cases ↔ consumer units ↔ pallets through the BOM levels (DU, CU, CUc) with scrap; (3) walk the time ladder from 8,760 hours to valuable operating time and compute OEE correctly; (4) run a rough-cut capacity check and list the levers for overload; (5) see how sequencing, frozen-zone changes and small lots create factory losses.
- Field: re-sequence one real week with the changeover matrix; RCCP for next month. KPI: output reliability, changeover minutes, DSR capacity gap costed. Forum includes a factory walk (or video walk) and the A1 charter clinic.

**P2 · Master Production Scheduling** 🛠 (Priority 3 · Cycle 5 · Jul–Aug, paired with P3)
- After this course you can: (1) explain MPS level and horizon (product group/month for 3–6 months vs DU/week near term) and time fences (frozen, slushy, liquid); (2) set lot sizes and campaigns (MOQ vs EOQ; batch = the larger of the two constraints); (3) sequence to cut changeovers (light → dark, mild → strong, same size together); (4) plan seasonal prebuild (Tết) against capacity and cash; (5) protect plan stability and read output reliability both ways (under and over).
- Field: build next month's MPS for one line in our MPS tool; count frozen-zone changes for 4 weeks. KPI: output reliability, frozen-zone changes, schedule adherence.

**P3 · Material Requirements Planning** 🛠 (Priority 3 · Cycle 5 · Jul–Aug, paired with P2)
- After this course you can: (1) net gross requirements (net = gross + safety stock − on hand − firm receipts); (2) apply MOQ and rounding to a planned order; (3) choose safety stock vs safety time (demand vs supply/lead-time variability); (4) read MRP exception messages and act on them; (5) manage phase-in/phase-out of RPM with a stock overview, including supplier feedstock.
- Field: master-data health check for your top 20 RPMs (lead time, MOQ, rounding, safety stock); one phase-out stock overview. KPI: line stops for materials (target 0), RPM cover days, MRP exception count.
- Forum (with P2): "Plan the line, feed the line" — one Tết-style scenario from MPS to purchase orders.

**P4 · Demand & the Business** 🛠 (Priority 3 · Cycle 6 · Aug–Sep)
- Demand-planning foundations taught through business acumen, plus reading the ML forecast (moved later because the ML forecast already runs).
- After this course you can: (1) turn volume into value (net sales value, gross margin, trade spend) and explain how a forecast miss hits the P&L, cash and service; (2) name the drivers of our demand (price, promotion, distribution, seasonality and Tết, channel GT/MT/e-commerce, launches) and log them; (3) measure accuracy and bias (lag-1 wMAPE, bias, tracking signal); (4) read what the ML forecast can and cannot see; (5) apply FVA and the five override rules (no small tweaks, careful going up, one owner, reason before number, review without blame).
- Field: bias root cause on your top 10 SKUs with a driver log; one cycle of override log with FVA. KPI: bias, lag-1 wMAPE, FVA ≥ 0, ≥ 80% of overrides logged with a reason.

**P5 · SLOB & Inventory Health** 🛠 (Priority 3 · Cycle 7 · Sep–Oct)
- After this course you can: (1) read inventory health (cover days, ageing, SLOB definition, provision vs write-off); (2) trace SLOB to root causes (over-buying, MOQ above usage, phase-outs, forecast bias, last-lot misses); (3) run the phase-out / last-lot process with a stock overview 3 months ahead; (4) choose the right lever (cut open orders, rework, re-allocate, liquidate, negotiate MOQ); (5) set prevention rules that stop SLOB at the source.
- Field: SLOB root-cause and action plan for your category; one phase-out stock overview. KPI: SLOB value, write-offs, cover days. Timed before year-end and the Tết 2028 build.

**P6 · Fill Rate & Customer Service** 🛠 (Priority 3 · Cycle 8 · Oct–Nov)
- After this course you can: (1) define fill rate, OTIF and case fill correctly (line, case, value; on time AND in full); (2) build the fill-rate loss tree (no stock, allocation cut, picking, late dispatch, transport, customer slot) and give each loss one owner; (3) allocate in shortage with an agreed rule and tell customers early; (4) run the S&OE rhythm (Wednesday loss-tree review, Thursday S&OE) with actions to closure; (5) explain why MT carries most of our lost sales and where the fixes are.
- Field: loss tree for your top MT accounts with one fix and an owner; S&OE action log for 4 weeks. KPI: MT fill rate, lost sales value. Tool: our Fill-rate & Service Loss-tree dashboard (model factory).
